2. STUDY PROGRAM AND COMMON PRINCIPLES
======================================================================

The manuscript contains:
- Study 1: random-intercept simulation with constant population slope across
  clusters.
- Study 2: random-intercept/random-slope simulation with genuine
  cluster-to-cluster slope heterogeneity.
- Study 3: empirical illustration using lme4::sleepstudy, handled under its
  separate prospective empirical plan and preserved Phase 6B freeze.

Studies 1 and 2 compare conventional mixed models, robust mixed models,
OLS/CR2, ordinary CATs, truncated CATs, and robust CATs. Truncated CATs is
retained as a prespecified negative-control/comparison method.

No method will be added, removed, tuned, rescued, or selected on the basis of
the definitive comparative results.

Common two-sided alpha:
  0.05

3. STUDY 1 — FROZEN DESIGN
======================================================================

Data-generating structure:
  out = intercept + beta * x + random_intercept + residual

Frozen values:
- clusters, G: 10, 20, 40
- observations per cluster: 40
- population slope beta: 0, 0.10
- fixed intercept: 0
- random-intercept SD: 1
- predictor SD: 1
- residual SD: 1
- contamination proportion: exactly 0.05 within each cluster
- attempted replications: 2,000 per condition
- conditions: 18

Contamination regimes:
1. Clean: no contamination.
2. Vertical: two of 40 observations per cluster; predictor unchanged; outcome
   displaced by 6 residual SDs with the prespecified symmetric sign mechanism.
3. Bad leverage: two of 40 observations per cluster; predictor set to the
   prespecified extreme magnitude of 4 predictor SDs and outcome displacement
   governed by the frozen 0.375 residual-SD bad-leverage rule.

Primary estimand:
  beta, the common population slope.

Study 1 method order:
1. ri               Conventional random-intercept LMM / Kenward-Roger
2. cr2              OLS / CR2 / Satterthwaite
3. cats             Ordinary CATs
4. cats_trunc       Truncated CATs
5. cats_robust      Robust CATs using robust::lmRob()
6. cats_robustbase  Robust CATs using robustbase::lmrob()
7. robust_ri        Robust random-intercept LMM / robust Satterthwaite

======================================================================

Data-generating structure:
  out = intercept + random_intercept +

Frozen values:
- clusters, G: 10, 20, 40
- observations per cluster: 40
- population mean slope beta: 0, 0.10
- fixed intercept: 0
- random-intercept SD: 1
- random-slope SD: 0.05, 0.10

- contamination regimes: clean and vertical

- attempted replications: 2,000 per condition

5. FROZEN ESTIMATION AND INFERENCE
======================================================================

Conventional random-intercept model:
  out ~ x + (1 | cluster)

Conventional independent random-slope model:
  out ~ x + (1 + x || cluster)

Conventional mixed-model estimation/inference:
- lmerTest::lmer()
- REML = TRUE
- package-default lme4 optimizer/control settings
- Kenward-Roger coefficient degrees of freedom
- t-based 95% confidence intervals

OLS/CR2:
- stats::lm(out ~ x)
- CR2 variance
- Satterthwaite coefficient inference and confidence intervals

Ordinary/truncated CATs:
- verified production implementation
- retained-cluster mean slope
- t reference with df = retained clusters - 1

Robust CATs:
- robust::lmRob() or robustbase::lmrob() within clusters using package defaults
- audited CATs aggregation/inference after cluster-level robust fits
- no robust-engine substitution or fallback

Robust mixed models:
- robustlmm::rlmer()
- method = "DAStau"
- setting = "RSEn"
- no customized robustness functions, tuning constants, optimizer rescue, or
  alternate robust setting
- fixed-effect inference from summary(fit, df = "satterthwaite")
- robustlmm default covariance
- t-based Wald 95% confidence interval

Study 1 robust mixed formula:
  out ~ x + (1 | cluster)

Study 2 robust random-intercept formula:
  out ~ x + (1 | cluster)

Study 2 robust independent random-slope formula:
  out ~ x + (1 + x || cluster)

6. CONVERGENCE, BOUNDARY, FAILURE, AND NO-RESCUE RULES
======================================================================

Conventional mixed models:
- a fit error, missing focal coefficient, nonfinite required inference, or
  genuine optimizer/convergence failure is a failed method result.
- the standard boundary-singularity message alone is not a convergence failure.
- a finite, otherwise converged boundary-singular fit remains in the primary
  analysis and is flagged separately.
- conventional singularity uses the verified 1e-4 tolerance.

Robust mixed models:
- a fit is usable only when robustlmm returns a fit, processFit supplies a
  finite convergence code equal to 0, and fixed-slope inference is complete and
  finite.
- robust boundary status is recorded using the validated theta/lower-bound rule
  at tolerance 1e-4.
- a converged finite robust boundary fit remains in the primary analysis.
- warnings/messages do not automatically invalidate an otherwise usable fit.

CR2:
- OLS and requested CR2 extraction must succeed with finite estimate, SE, df,
  p value, and CI, with SE > 0 and df > 0.

CATs methods:
- at least two retained cluster slopes are required and aggregate inference must
  be finite.
- robust-CATs warning-bearing cluster fits are retained when required
  coefficients are finite; fitting errors, missing coefficients, and nonfinite
  coefficients cause the affected cluster to be dropped under the verified
  production rule.
- retained-cluster counts determine CATs degrees of freedom.

No-rescue rule:
- failed fits are not replaced.
- no alternate optimizer, RSEa refit, alternate robust setting, fallback
  variance estimator, or robust-engine substitution is used after a failure.
- method-specific usable denominators and failure rates are reported.

8. DETERMINISTIC RANDOM-NUMBER DESIGN
======================================================================

The definitive simulation uses common random numbers within cluster-count
blocks.

Study 1 condition seed blocks:
- G = 10: 20260815
- G = 20: 20260816
- G = 40: 20260817

All six Study 1 conditions with a given G use the same 2,000-replication seed
vector.

Study 2 condition seed blocks:
- G = 10: 20260905
- G = 20: 20260906
- G = 40: 20260907

All eight Study 2 conditions with a given G use the same 2,000-replication seed
vector.

For every cluster-count block, the exact replication seed vector is generated
monolithically as:

  set.seed(condition_seed)
  sample.int(
    .Machine$integer.max,
    2000,
    replace = FALSE
  )

The prospective freeze archives the complete six 2,000-replication seed vectors
and validates them against this deterministic rule.

Method-specific seeds are derived deterministically from each replication seed
using the already-validated package implementation so method results remain
reproducible and invariant to requested method order.

The freeze records RNGkind() and the relevant R/software environment. The RNG
state of the caller is restored after seed-vector construction during freeze
preparation.

9. SHARDING, CHECKPOINTS, RESUME, AND DISK RULES
======================================================================

Production shard size:
  10 replications

Shards per 2,000-replication condition:
  200

Rules:
- sharding receives the exact frozen subset of the monolithic 2,000-rep seed
  vector; shards do not regenerate shorter seed vectors.
- each shard checkpoint records condition ID, shard ID, replicate range,
  replication seeds, method schedule, status, timing, and fitting output.
- completed compatible shard checkpoints are reused.
- an existing checkpoint that does not match the frozen condition/shard/seeds/
  method specification is refused rather than silently reused.
- a matching error checkpoint may be retried using the exact same frozen
  specification; this is recovery from interrupted/failed computation, not
  replacement of a failed method fit.
- atomic write/replace logic is used for restartable artifacts.
- before a new shard begins, at least 2 GB free disk space must be verified.
  Failure to determine free disk space stops new work.
- after a complete condition checkpoint has been written and verified,
  temporary completed shard files may be removed under the frozen
  retain_completed_shards = FALSE rule to limit disk use.
- complete condition checkpoints are the durable simulation artifacts.

The definitive runs are serial/local unless a separately documented prospective
amendment changes execution scheduling without changing scientific results.
